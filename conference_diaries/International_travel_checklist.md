# WIP: Checklist: International travel for conferences

<i>Sharing all the guidance that was once shared with me, plus some of my own insights :)</i>

## TL;DR

- passport
- visa
- zero-forex-card or international card or e wallet
- e sim or international roaming
- local currency
- International travel adapter
- language translator app
- AI for translating big documents (image to translated text)
- download google maps offline
- hard copy of all important documents 
- box of medicines + essentials
- some food and water
- a local person's contact

## Expanding on above points

- passport 
- visa 
    - check if you need one: "does a [your nationality] passport holder need a visa to enter [your destination] transiting via [transit country] for [layover time] hours?"
    - what type of visa do you need? eg.: for conferences it's usually Schengen Type C for 29 European countries-- there are also e-visas or visa-on-arrival for some countries.
    - always refer to latest and official governnment webpages and resources only!
    - vfs global (https://visa.vfsglobal.com/india/) - for schengen visa:
        - there is usually a document checklist-- you have to bring all those at the day of your appointment + a cover letter (you can use an LLM to write that)
        - book fully refundable flight tickets (eg emirates) and hotel (eg booking.com)-- and remember to cancel them on time if the visa decision takes too long. once you have your visa you can rebook cheaper flights (eg: skyscanner)
        - get health insurance only from companies listed by vfs-global
        - I would recommend putting extra documents to support that you have strong ties to your home country, if you are applying for the first time.. and you have a weak passport. example: NOC from college/company, any property that you own in your country, any dependents, any letter of support from your community's members etc.
    - once you get the visa, understand the terms of your visa; i.e. start date, end date, purpose of visit, number of entries, etc. and do not violate them.
    - [By Sanket Verma - US-visa specific] They mostly look for:
        - A valid reason to visit the country
        - Having enough financial means to support yourself when travelling in case you miss a flight or something happens
        - Ties to your home country
        - No intent of immigrating illegally
    - US visa process is interview based. Here you can read other people's interview experiences: https://www.reddit.com/r/usvisascheduling/
    - US immigration law Section 214(b) presumes every visa applicant is an intending immigrant until they prove otherwise-- it is a very common reason of denial -- and you have 2-2.5 mins to prove otherwise! Refer: https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/visa-denials.html
- card that can do international payments - if possible enable contactless payments with it-- try making an international payment before you leave your country-- just to confirm. I have had good experience with this one - https://goniyo.com/zero-forex-card . Some people also have apple e-wallets for mobile payments.
- esim or international roaming: depends on the place you are visiting -- sometimes the best option is to buy a local sim as well
    - esim: https://www.airalo.com/ -- this might be banned in a few countries. I haven't had a great experience with esims-- also if you have an old phone there is a high chance that the esim won't work. Make sure your phone supports the esim you are buying, before buying the esim.
    - carry the small pin to open your phone's sim slot: because sometimes you have to take out your sim and put it back to activate the international roaming after entering your destination.
    - before getting international roaming make sure your sim company (like airtel, vodafone, jio, etc.) provides coverage in the country you are travelling to.
- some local currency-- if you can't get it then at least have Euros or USD -- they can be converted at most of the airports-- sometimes airports also have machines in which you can put your 0-forex-card and get out the local currency -- also get the receipt from the place you convert(especially if you are not converting at airport) so that you have proof if the currency turns out to be fake.
- International travel adapter aka universal charger
- language translator app that runs without internet - you can download languages on google translator app
- any AI to translate bigger text documents.. like if they tell you to sign something during immigration.. you can click the picture of the document and ask the LLM to translate it all. (example: chatgpt, google lens)
- download google maps offline of the area you would be staying at.. and the route from airport to your hotel at least. 
- have hard copy of all documents in a folder from most to least important as per the immigration questioning(passport, purpose of visit (your conference invitation letter), number of days of stay, hotel booking, next connecting flight and return flight, bank statements, and all other documents).
- a box of medicines, bandages, essentials, etc.
- some food - nuts, trail mix, protein bars and water
- have a local person's contact number-- in case of emergencies-- can be a conference organiser. also share your health insurance and your house contact with at least one person at the conference-- can be a conference organiser.

## Some extra "good-to-have"s

- any other app that's local to that region and essential - for weather, bus routes, etc.
- compression socks, earplugs and neck pillow, if the flight is too long -- and some toffees as you can get nauseated; and alcohol wipes to sanitise your seat and surroundings!
- a thin blanket or a comfy hoodie, if you have a long layover
- if your flight gets delayed and you have a following connecting flight then you can go to your airlines booth and they will put you on the next flight-- that's why it is recommended you buy flights with a single ticket number.
- sometimes you can find vlogs of people guiding how to transit through an airport-- that can be helpful -- so that you don't get lost at your transit-- but make sure it is a recent video-- also sometimes you can find the maps of the airport online as well, with all the transit instructions marked...
- keep images of all receipts, invoices etc in a folder in your phone, if you are going to reimburse them later.
- booking a cab in advance is usually cheaper than booking on the spot-- and you can switch to "business" mode (in uber) to later get an invoice.
- for checking flight and aircraft details(boeing/airbus, age of plane etc.): https://www.flightaware.com/ , https://www.flightradar24.com/
- india specific - fast track immigration: https://ftittp.mha.gov.in/fti/
- And yes, visa/immigration systems are pretty racist! So if you are a weak passport holder, no matter how things turn out, don't forget to treat yourself afterwards :)

PS: This checklist is primarily designed from the perspective of an able-bodied, neurotypical traveler, with a strong focus on Indian travelers going to European/US destinations-- reflecting my own experiences and those of people within similar contexts. But, travel needs and requirements vary greatly across different abilities, cultures, and backgrounds. So, if you'd like to make this list more inclusive and diverse, I would welcome and encourge you to submit your suggestions via a PR or by creating an issue [here](https://github.com/Schefflera-Arboricola/blogs/issues/new). Thank you :)
